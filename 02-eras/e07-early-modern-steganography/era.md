# 近代早期与隐写（Early Modern & Steganography）

> 1600–1789 · 轴1 编年叙事

**中文**：这一代的主角不是「改字母」而是「藏信息」。隐写（steganography）指把通信本身的存在藏起来——培根的双字体（字母 A/B 用印刷粗细两种字形编码，普通读者只看到一篇散文）是经典例子；卡当格栅（在一张打孔的卡片上按孔位置写字，收信人把卡片对齐才能读出隐藏的行）把密文藏进无害的纸面。书密码（以某本书为密钥，密文是页码行号）也在此期成熟。同时，欧洲外交的「名册密码」（nomenclator，常用词配码）仍是主流，频率分析被「同音码」（给高频字母分配多个替身符号）部分化解。这一代证明了一件事：**「有没有通信」本身也可以是秘密**，这条「藏而不掩」的路线与「改字母」的路线从此并行。

**English**: The star of this generation was not "changing letters" but "hiding information." Steganography means hiding the *existence* of communication itself — Bacon’s biliteral cipher (letters A/B encoded by two font weights, so ordinary readers see only plain prose) is the classic case; the cardan grille (write letters through holes in a punched card, and only the aligned card reveals the hidden rows) hides the message inside an innocent sheet. Book ciphers (a book as key, ciphertext is page-and-line numbers) also matured here. Meanwhile, Europe’s diplomatic "nomenclator" (common words as codes) remained the mainstream, and frequency analysis was partly deflected by "homophones" (multiple aliases for high-frequency letters). This generation proved a point: **whether communication happens at all can itself be a secret** — the "hide, don’t alter" route now runs in parallel with the "alter letters" route.
